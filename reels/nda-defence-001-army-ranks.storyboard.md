## 1. The idea
- One sentence: an Indian Army officer climbs 9 ranks from Lieutenant (where every NDA cadet starts after IMA) to General,
  and each rank is read off the shoulder by a simple code: stars, then the national emblem, then the crossed sword and
  baton; Colonel and Brigadier differ by one star.
- End-card takeaway: "9 ranks. Lieutenant to General." (5 words). Label: NDA · ARMY RANKS.
- Source: the founder's brief; L8 (the account's biggest reels were defence content, not maths). No PYQ (not a maths
  reel; no bank question is named, so no claim is made).

## 2. The viewer
- NDA aspirant, 16–19, scrolling, often muted. Knows "stars on the shoulder" and that a General is the top; most can't
  tell Colonel from Brigadier, or name what sits between Major and General.
- Likely answers to frame 0 (emblem, one star, two stars side by side): **A Colonel** (the strong trap: "emblem + stars =
  Colonel", the rank they've heard most, and the badge also has the emblem and stars); **C Major General** (the "it has
  the most on it, it must be senior" pull). Right: **B Brigadier** (three stars; Colonel has two).

## 3. The question (frame 0)
- Label: `NDA · ARMY RANKS`. Lines: "Which rank wears this?" (22 chars) / "Look at the shoulder." (21 chars).
- Options: A Colonel · B Brigadier · C Major Gen (chips 290 wide: "B  Brigadier" is the widest, 12 chars × 42 px × 0.55 ≈
  280 px).
- On frame 0: the strap, wearing the national emblem over three stars in a triangle (one star, then two side by side),
  close enough that the three stars are counted at a glance (each star ≥ 80 px across at the front).
- Answerable in 2 s by anyone who knows the code; for the rest it's a guess the reel settles, which is the hook.

## 4. Frame 0 and the cover
- Frame 0: the strap from 25° off its front, the orbit at speed (fast through the back half, slow through the front, so
  the badges face the viewer for most of the 3 s), the brass catching a moving highlight. Header up.
- Cover: t = 2.7 s (orbit near the front, nothing answered: the chips not lit, tested). Cover-only line "Which rank
  wears this?" at y 1590 (inside the 3:4 crop 240–1680); the strap fills the middle.

## 7. The story without words
A shoulder strap with badges turns. One option lights; its three stars are joined into a triangle. One star slides
away: two left, a different badge. Then the strap is cleared and badges land one at a time, more and more, symbols
changing from stars to an emblem to a sword and baton, while a list on the left lights up row by row, climbing. At the
top, a wreath badge. The rank names carry the meaning (they are the picture's labels, like a graph's axis), so muted
viewers lose nothing.

## 8. Honest facts (sources, read 2026-10-27)
- Order of ranks, Lieutenant → General; General is the Army Chief's rank (also held by a Chief of Defence Staff from the
  Army, so the reel doesn't say "only one"); Second Lieutenant no longer used, "all new officers are commissioned as
  Lieutenants"; Field Marshal ceremonial, only two holders: Wikipedia, *Indian Army ranks and insignia*, *Field marshal
  (India)* (Manekshaw 1973, Cariappa 1986; only the years go on screen: the reviewer found 1 Jan 1973 / 15 Jan 1986 as the
  official dates and 3 Jan / 28 Apr as the ceremonies).
- Each badge checked by eye against the Wikimedia Commons insignia pictures used by that article (2026-10-27), and in
  words where a page has them: Lieutenant 2 stars in a column; Captain 3 stars in a column; Major the emblem; Lt Colonel
  the emblem over 1 star; Colonel "two five-pointed stars and the National emblem above" (*Colonel (India)*), in a column;
  Brigadier "the National emblem over three five-pointed stars in a triangular formation" (*Brigadier (India)*): one star,
  then two side by side; Major General "a crossed sword and baton and a five-pointed star above" (*Major general
  (India)*); Lieutenant General "a crossed sword and baton and Ashoka emblem above" (*Lieutenant general (India)*);
  General "crossed sword and baton with a five-pointed star and Ashoka emblem above" (*General (India)*).
- Field Marshal: the emblem over **two crossed batons** in a wreath. *Field marshal (India)*: "two crossed batons
  surrounded by an oak wreath, with the Ashoka Lion placed above"; the Commons picture was corrected to two batons on
  31 Jan 2024 "based on Sam Manekshaw uniform". Some exam sites say "a baton and sabre in a lotus wreath" (older text);
  we follow the uniform. The wreath's leaves are drawn plainly and not named on screen.
- "9 ranks": Lt, Capt, Maj, Lt Col, Col, Brig, Maj Gen, Lt Gen, Gen (tested: the ladder has 9 rows below FM).
- The emblem is drawn as a simplified gold lion capital, only as part of the badges, never as our mark or near any
  brand (State Emblem of India (Prohibition of Improper Use) Act, 2005). No words on it, no "Satyameva Jayate".

## 9. Risks
- **The emblem reads as a blob on a phone.** Big (≥ 130 px tall at the front), simple: three lion heads, the abacus, the
  wheel, in raised brass with a dark edge.
- **The sword and baton read as a plain X.** A curved sabre with a hilt and a straight baton with end caps, different
  thicknesses.
- **Lt Gen vs Gen look the same.** The star drops in between, centre frame, with its own tink.
- **Too fast to read.** 1.1 s per rank: the rank's name shows big under the strap as its badge lands, and its ladder row
  stays lit; the whole ladder is in view again in the Field Marshal shot.
- **Colour-blind:** the lit row is orange on white with a marker dot and bold weight, not colour alone.
- **The opening orbit shows the strap edge-on or from behind**: the orbit runs fast through the back half and slow
  through the front, so the badges face the viewer most of the 3 s.
- **The emblem law:** see § 8.
