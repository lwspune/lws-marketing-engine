## 1. The idea
- One sentence: an Indian Army officer climbs 9 ranks from Lieutenant (where every NDA cadet starts after IMA) to General,
  and each rank is read off the shoulder by a simple code: stars, then the national emblem, then the crossed sword and
  baton; Colonel and Brigadier differ by one star and its triangle.
- End-card takeaway: "9 ranks. Lieutenant to General." (5 words). Label: NDA · ARMY RANKS.
- Source: the founder's brief; L8 (the account's biggest reels were defence content, not maths). No PYQ (not a maths
  reel; SSB and GAT aspirants are asked ranks, but no bank question is named, so no claim is made).

## 2. The viewer
- NDA aspirant, 16–19, scrolling, often muted. Knows "stars on the shoulder" and that a General is the top; most can't
  tell Colonel from Brigadier, or name what sits between Major and General.
- Likely answers to frame 0 (emblem + three stars in a triangle): **A Colonel** (the strong trap: "emblem + stars =
  Colonel", the rank they've heard most, and the badge also has the emblem and stars); **C Major General** (the "it has
  the most on it, it must be senior" pull). Right: **B Brigadier** (the three stars in a triangle; Colonel has two).

## 3. The question (frame 0)
- Label: `NDA · ARMY RANKS`. Lines: "Which rank wears this?" (22 chars) / "Look at the shoulder." (21 chars).
- Options: A Colonel · B Brigadier · C Major Gen (chips 290 wide: "B  Brigadier" is the widest, 12 chars × 42 px × 0.55 ≈
  280 px incl. letter).
- On frame 0: the strap, wearing the national emblem over three stars in a triangle, already in the orbit, close enough
  that the three stars are counted at a glance (each star ≥ 90 px across at the front).
- Answerable in 2 s by anyone who knows the code; for the rest it's a guess the reel settles, which is the hook.

## 4. Frame 0 and the cover

  highlight. Header up.
- Cover: t = 2.7 s (orbit back near the front, nothing answered: the chips not lit, tested). Cover-only line "Which rank
  wears this?" at y 1590 (inside the 3:4 crop 240–1680); the strap fills the middle.

## 7. The story without words
A shoulder strap with badges turns. One option lights; its three stars are joined into a triangle. One star slides
away: two left, a different badge. Then the strap is cleared and badges land one at a time, more and more, symbols
changing from stars to an emblem to a sword and baton, while a list on the left lights up row by row, climbing. At the
top, a wreath badge. The rank names carry the meaning (they are the picture's labels, like a graph's axis), so muted
viewers lose nothing.

## 8. Honest facts (sources, read 2026-10-27)
- Order of ranks, Lieutenant → General, General held only by the Chief of the Army Staff; Second Lieutenant no longer
  used, "all new officers are commissioned as Lieutenants"; Field Marshal ceremonial, only two holders: Wikipedia,
  *Indian Army ranks and insignia*, *Field marshal (India)* (Manekshaw 3 Jan 1973; Cariappa 28 Apr 1986).
- Colonel: "two five-pointed stars and the National emblem above" (*Colonel (India)*). Brigadier: "the National emblem
  over three five-pointed stars in a triangular formation" (*Brigadier (India)*). Major General: "a crossed sword and
  baton and a five-pointed star above" (*Major general (India)*). Lieutenant General: "a crossed sword and baton and
  Ashoka emblem above" (*Lieutenant general (India)*). Field Marshal: "two crossed batons surrounded by an oak wreath,
  with the Ashoka Lion placed above the wreath" (*Field marshal (India)*). Major: the emblem replaced the British crown
  for Majors and above in 1950 (*Major (India)*).
- General (the Army Chief): "Crossed sword and baton with a five-pointed star and Ashoka emblem above" (*General
  (India)*). Lieutenant 2 stars, Captain "three five-pointed stars" (the insignia table mirrored from Wikipedia on the
  military-history wiki).
- Not yet quoted: Lieutenant Colonel, the emblem over 1 star (between Major's emblem alone and Colonel's emblem over 2
  stars, the British crown-and-pip system with India's emblem). **Confirm against an official picture before the code.**
- "9 ranks": Lt, Capt, Maj, Lt Col, Col, Brig, Maj Gen, Lt Gen, Gen (tested: the ladder has 9 rows below FM).
- The emblem is drawn as a simplified gold lion capital, only as part of the badges, never as our mark or near any
  brand (State Emblem of India (Prohibition of Improper Use) Act, 2005). No words on it, no "Satyameva Jayate".

## 9. Risks
- **The emblem reads as a blob on a phone.** Big (≥ 150 px tall at the front), simple: three lion heads, the abacus, the
  wheel, in raised brass with a dark edge.
- **The sword and baton read as a plain X.** A curved sabre with a hilt and a straight baton with end caps, different
  thicknesses.
- **Lt Gen vs Gen look the same.** The star drops in between, centre frame, with its own tink.
- **Too fast to read.** 1.1 s per rank: the ladder row lights as the badge lands, the name stays lit, so it can be read
  back; the whole ladder is visible again in the Field Marshal shot.
- **Colour-blind:** the lit row is orange on white with a marker dot and bold weight, not colour alone.
- **The opening orbit shows the strap edge-on** (a thin slab): it passes through in under 0.4 s; the brass relief
  catches the light.
- **The emblem law:** see § 8.
