## 1. The idea
- One sentence: the Indian Air Force's four front-line fighters as a line-up you can tell apart: **Tejas** (a small triangle
  wing with a kink), **Mirage 2000** (a triangle wing with a straight edge), **Su-30MKI** (two tail fins) and **Rafale** (front
  wings, one fin). "Triangle wing", not "delta" (review I1).
- Why: aspirants love jets; the tour's jet shot was the panel's best moment; a line-up is saved and shared ("which one will
  you fly?" in the caption, not on screen).

## 2. The viewer
- NDA aspirant 16–19, often muted, Air Force hopefuls especially. Knows the names, can't tell the shapes apart.

## 3. Frame 0
- Header: `DEFENCE · IAF JETS`, then "4 fighter jets of the" / "Indian Air Force", then four numbered dots (1–4): the jet
  being shown fills green, shown ones go dark.
- Picture: four jets in a diamond (Rafale ahead, Tejas left, Mirage right, Su-30MKI behind) seen ¾ from above-behind,
  clouds streaming past far below, a number tag on each (1–4, matching the dots); the camera turning at speed. The jets
  are already part-lit (dark grey, a third of the way to their real colour: review I2), fully lit at their visit.

## 4. Cover
- t = 3.2 s (before the first glide): the four part-lit jets with their tags. **Cover only:** the header is hidden and the
  promise, "4 fighter jets of the / Indian Air Force", sits on the board at y ≈ 262–458, above the picture band (470) and
  inside the 3:4 crop (240–1680) (review B4).

## 7. The story without words

that makes it different. One rolls. All four roar overhead and climb away.

## 9. Risks
- **Tejas and Mirage look alike** (both tailless deltas, one engine). That is the lesson: the ring on the Tejas's kinked
  leading edge, then the Mirage's straight one; the Tejas is smaller.
- **28 s is long.** A new jet every 4.5 s, the dots ticking, the roll and the fly-over at the end.
- **A toy look.** Bevelled shapes, glass canopies, real greys, glowing nozzles.
