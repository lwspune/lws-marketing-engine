## The question (copy exactly)

> What is ∫₁² xˣ (1 + ln x) dx equal to?
> (a) 4  (b) 3  (c) 2  (d) 1

## Why this one

It looks like a monster integral, but it's a 10-second question once you spot that the integrand is
the derivative of xˣ. Students who try to integrate it directly lose minutes. "Check whether it's a
derivative first" is a habit worth teaching, so the reel is useful, not just clever.

## Paper cheat sheet (copy from this while filming)

```
Q. ∫₁² xˣ (1 + ln x) dx = ?
   (a) 4   (b) 3   (c) 2   (d) 1

Let y = xˣ
ln y = x ln x
(1/y) dy/dx = ln x + 1
dy/dx = xˣ (1 + ln x)        ← same as the integrand!

∫₁² xˣ(1 + ln x) dx = [ xˣ ]₁²
                    = 2² − 1¹
                    = 4 − 1 = 3        Ans: (b)
```
